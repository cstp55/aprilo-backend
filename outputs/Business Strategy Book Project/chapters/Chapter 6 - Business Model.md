# Chapter 6 - Business Model

## Executive Summary

The company should operate as a SaaS platform with capability-based packaging, usage-aware expansion, premium connectors, implementation services, and a future marketplace. Pricing should not be based on prompts, embeddings, or raw model calls. Customers should pay for business value: enabled capabilities, connected systems, employees served, workflows executed, and measurable outcomes.

The model should begin simple for HRMS and become more sophisticated as the platform expands. Early revenue should come from paid pilots and monthly subscriptions. Later revenue can include premium integrations, multi-system workflow packs, enterprise security, partner implementation, and a business capability marketplace.

The business model goal is to become software, not services. Services may be necessary early for learning and onboarding, but productized deployment, reusable connectors, and capability packs must become the economic engine.

## Research Findings

### 1. Customers increasingly demand measurable AI ROI.

McKinsey and Deloitte both show that organizations are using AI widely but still face value realization and scaling challenges. This means pricing should be tied to business outcomes or clear operating value rather than technical consumption.

### 2. SaaS buyers understand subscriptions, but AI cost introduces usage risk.

Traditional SaaS pricing commonly uses seats, tiers, modules, or usage metrics. AI products add variable inference, retrieval, storage, and workflow execution costs. A healthy business model must prevent gross margin leakage through uncontrolled AI usage.

### 3. Capability-based packaging fits the product philosophy.

The Company Blueprint says customers should not need to understand prompts, vector databases, embeddings, or tool calling. That same principle should shape pricing. Customers should buy "HR Knowledge Assistant" or "Employee Request Automation," not "500K tokens."

### 4. Connectors can become monetizable value.

Integrations into HRMS, ecommerce, ERP, CRM, and support platforms create customer value and implementation complexity. Premium connectors can justify higher plans, especially when they enable write actions, webhooks, permissions, and audit logs.

### 5. Services should be used strategically.

Early implementation services can help learn customer workflows and reduce onboarding friction. However, services should not become the core revenue model unless they directly create reusable product assets.

## Business Model Architecture

| Revenue Stream | Description | Timing | Strategic Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform subscription | Monthly/annual base fee for workspace, security, knowledge, and AI capability platform | Start | Core recurring revenue |
| Capability packs | Paid modules such as HR Knowledge, Onboarding, Policy Assistant, Ecommerce Order Intelligence | Start | Aligns price with business value |
| Premium connectors | Advanced integrations to HRMS, ecommerce, ERP, CRM, databases, helpdesk systems | Year 1-2 | Monetizes integration depth |
| Usage expansion | AI actions, workflow executions, employee volume, knowledge volume, API calls | Year 1-2 | Captures scale without punishing experimentation |
| Implementation services | Setup, knowledge cleanup, workflow mapping, integration support | Start | Helps early adoption and learning |
| Enterprise edition | SSO, audit logs, private deployment, advanced permissions, custom retention | Year 2+ | Higher ACV and trust |
| Partner revenue | Revenue share, reseller margin, implementation certification | Year 2+ | Scalable distribution |
| Capability marketplace | Third-party or internal capability packs | Year 3+ | Ecosystem optionality |

## Pricing Hypothesis

These are modeled assumptions and must be validated through customer interviews and paid pilots.

| Plan | Target Customer | Price Hypothesis | Includes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Starter | Small business HR team | $199-$499/month | HR knowledge assistant, limited employees, basic document sources |
| Growth | SMB/mid-market HR team | $799-$1,500/month | HR knowledge, onboarding support, ticket deflection, basic integrations |
| Scale | Mid-market operations | $2,000-$5,000/month | Multiple capabilities, premium connectors, workflow routing, audit logs |
| Enterprise | Larger regulated organizations | Custom | SSO, custom deployment, governance, SLA, security review, advanced integrations |

## Packaging Principles

1. Price around business capabilities, not AI mechanics.

2. Keep the first purchase simple: one business problem, one capability pack, one clear ROI metric.

3. Use annual contracts where possible after pilot validation.

4. Use implementation fees when setup requires workflow mapping or integration work.

5. Protect margins with fair-use limits, usage controls, caching, model routing, and tiered workflow volumes.

6. Avoid heavy customization unless the output becomes reusable product capability.

## Unit Economics Logic

| Metric | Target Direction | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 70%-85% long-term | Needed for SaaS economics after AI/cloud costs |
| CAC payback | Under 12 months for SMB/mid-market once repeatable | Indicates efficient acquisition |
| Net revenue retention | Above 100% after expansion packs | Shows platform expansion |
| Implementation time | Under 1 day for simple HR knowledge MVP; under 2 weeks for deeper workflows | Keeps sales and onboarding scalable |
| AI cost as revenue share | Target below 10%-20% after optimization | Prevents margin risk |
| Partner-sourced revenue | Meaningful by Year 2 | Reduces founder-led sales dependency |

## Strategic Analysis

The business model must match the product thesis. If the product claims to make AI business-ready, pricing must also feel business-ready. Customers should understand what they are buying in operational terms.

The best early model is a subscription plus capability pack structure. This lets the company start narrow while leaving room for expansion:

- Customer starts with HR Knowledge Assistant.
- Adds Onboarding Assistant.
- Adds HR Ticket Deflection.
- Adds premium HRMS connector.
- Adds employee self-service portal or Slack/Teams interface.
- Expands to other departments.

This creates land-and-expand revenue without requiring the company to sell an overly broad platform on day one.

## Founder Decisions

1. Use capability-based pricing as the primary model.

2. Avoid pricing primarily by tokens, prompts, or embeddings.

3. Charge implementation fees when setup work is meaningful, but productize every repeatable onboarding step.

4. Use annual contracts after proof-of-value pilots.

5. Build margin controls from day one: model routing, usage limits, caching, retrieval optimization, and workflow throttles.

6. Design partner economics by Year 2 so agencies and consultants can make money implementing the platform.

## Open Questions

1. What starting price will HRMS buyers accept before ROI proof?

2. Should paid pilots be fixed-fee, monthly, or refundable against annual contract?

3. Which pricing metric is easiest for customers to understand: employees, capabilities, connected systems, workflows, or usage?

4. Should implementation services be mandatory for the first 20 customers?

5. What gross margin is realistic before AI optimization?

6. How should partner revenue share work without damaging margins?
