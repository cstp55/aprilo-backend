# Chapter 2 Market Opportunity - Research Plan

## Working Thesis

Chapter 2 should prove that the company is entering the market at the right time: enterprise software is moving from systems of record and SaaS dashboards toward AI-enabled workflow execution. The chapter should validate HRMS as the fastest beachhead, then show how the same operational AI platform can expand into ecommerce, ERP, CRM, manufacturing, healthcare, and other integration-heavy industries.

## Chapter 2 Evidence Standard

Every quantitative claim should be tagged as one of four evidence types:

1. Primary source: vendor filings, investor relations, official product pages, public APIs, government or OECD data.
2. Analyst source: McKinsey, IDC, Gartner public pages, Deloitte, BCG, Forrester public materials.
3. Market infrastructure source: BuiltWith, W3Techs, Datanyze, Stack Overflow, GitHub, package/plugin marketplaces, app stores.
4. Modeled assumption: our own TAM/SAM/SOM estimate, clearly documented with formula, assumptions, confidence level, and source trail.

No number should enter the final chapter without:

- Source URL
- Publication date or access date
- Geography covered
- Segment covered
- Confidence rating: High / Medium / Low
- Notes on limitations

## 2.2 Macro Market

Purpose: Explain where software is going.

Topics to validate:

- SaaS evolution from seats and dashboards toward outcomes and automation
- Cloud and API maturity as the integration foundation
- Enterprise software fragmentation across HRMS, ERP, CRM, ecommerce, support, finance, and operations
- Generative AI adoption moving from experimentation to workflow redesign
- Agentic AI adoption and governance gap
- Workflow automation and no-code/low-code demand

Priority sources:

- McKinsey State of AI 2025
- Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025/2026
- IDC worldwide AI and software spending forecasts
- Deloitte State of AI in the Enterprise 2026
- OECD AI policy/adoption reports
- AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, and Oracle AI platform materials
- Public Gartner commentary where available

Required outputs:

- Macro trend timeline: Cloud -> SaaS -> API economy -> GenAI -> Agentic/Operational AI
- "Why now" table
- Market forces chart

## 2.5 Operational AI Opportunity

Purpose: Define the company category.

Positioning to validate:

- Not a chatbot
- Not a generic LLM wrapper
- Not only a developer agent framework
- Operational AI: AI that understands business context, integrates with systems, executes tasks, respects permissions, and measures outcomes

Evidence to collect:

- Analyst commentary on workflow redesign and AI value realization
- AI pilot failure / scaling friction evidence
- Examples of agentic AI moving into customer support, IT, knowledge management, operations, finance, and manufacturing
- Governance and reliability concerns that create demand for controlled operational layers

Required outputs:

- Category definition
- "Chatbot vs Copilot vs Agent Builder vs Operational AI" comparison
- Strategic implication: integration-first products should outperform standalone chat interfaces

## 2.8 TAM

Purpose: Build a defensible bottom-up total addressable market.

Preferred formula:

Potential businesses using target operational platforms x expected annual contract value = TAM

TAM should be calculated in layers:

- HRMS operational AI TAM
- Ecommerce operational AI TAM
- ERP operational AI TAM
- CRM operational AI TAM
- Cross-platform operational AI TAM

Data needed:

- Number of target businesses/platform installs
- Percentage with integration-ready systems
- Business size segments
- Estimated ACV by segment
- Expansion revenue assumptions

Required outputs:

- TAM formula table
- Assumption log
- Sensitivity model: conservative / base / aggressive

## 2.9 SAM

Purpose: Narrow TAM into the reachable serviceable available market.

Filters:

- Geography: start with India/global English-speaking SMB and mid-market, then expand
- Industry: HRMS first, then ecommerce and ERP
- Company size: SMB and mid-market first; enterprise later
- Integration readiness: platforms with APIs and active partner ecosystems
- Language: English first, multilingual/regional language expansion later
- Deployment: cloud and API-accessible systems first

Required outputs:

- SAM filter waterfall
- Segment definition
- First 24-month market scope

## 2.10 SOM

Purpose: Convert the market into a realistic capture plan.

Model:

- Year 1: pilot and early paid customers
- Year 2: repeatable HRMS playbook
- Year 3: partner-led expansion and ecommerce pilots
- Year 5: multi-platform operational AI suite

Inputs:

- Number of customers
- ACV
- Conversion rate
- Churn
- Expansion revenue
- Partner channel contribution
- Implementation capacity

Required outputs:

- SOM by year
- Revenue range by year
- Assumption confidence
- Investor-ready explanation of why the capture rate is realistic

## 2.12 Strategic Conclusion

Purpose: End like a consulting chapter.

Required structure:

- Findings
- Supporting evidence
- Strategic implications
- Open questions
- Final decisions

Expected decisions to test:

- Decision 1: Start with HRMS.
- Decision 2: Build platform-first, not single-use-case-first.
- Decision 3: Prioritize integration-ready platforms.
- Decision 4: Use partner-led GTM through HR consultants, implementation partners, agencies, and platform ecosystems.
- Decision 5: Measure ROI through resolved requests, time saved, automation completed, support deflection, and workflow cycle-time reduction.

- Build TAM/SAM/SOM model from bottom-up assumptions.
- Create conservative/base/aggressive scenarios.

Phase 5 - Chapter drafting:

- Draft each section in sequence.
- Add tables/charts as exhibits.
- End with final decisions and open research questions.

Phase 6 - Validation:

- Check every number against source.
- Remove or label weak assumptions.
- Make sure each strategic decision follows from evidence.
