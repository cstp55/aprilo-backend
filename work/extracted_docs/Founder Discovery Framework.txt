[Heading 1] Founder Discovery Framework (FDF)
[Heading 2] Version
[First Paragraph] 0.1
[Heading 2] Purpose
[First Paragraph] The Founder Discovery Framework (FDF) defines how the founding team will validate ideas before product development.
[Body Text] The objective is not to prove that our idea is correct.
[Body Text] The objective is to discover reality.
[Body Text] Every assumption must be supported by evidence before it becomes a product requirement.
[Heading 1] Company Mission
[First Paragraph] Build the world’s most trusted AI Operating Layer for Businesses.
[Heading 1] Discovery Philosophy
[First Paragraph] We do not build features.
[Body Text] We solve validated business problems.
[Body Text] Every feature must answer three questions.
[Compact] What customer problem does this solve?
[Compact] How frequently does the problem occur?
[Compact] Why will someone pay for this solution?
[Heading 1] Founder Success Metrics
[First Paragraph] The research phase is successful if we discover:
[Body Text] • Top 20 customer pain points.
[Body Text] • Top 10 workflow bottlenecks.
[Body Text] • Top 10 reasons customers reject existing AI products.
[Body Text] • Top 20 feature gaps.
[Body Text] • Top 10 measurable ROI opportunities.

[Heading 1] Exit Criteria
[First Paragraph] The company proceeds to PRD creation only if:
[Normal] The top ten customer pain points are consistently validated.
[Normal] At least five design partners agree to evaluate the MVP.
[Normal] A clear product differentiation exists.
[Normal] Customers can quantify ROI.
[Normal] Customers express willingness to pay.
[Normal] The founding team identifies a defendable market position.
[Normal] The product vision is supported by evidence rather than assumptions.
